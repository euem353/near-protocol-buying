## What you're buying when you buy NEAR

NEAR Protocol is a Layer 1 blockchain that launched its mainnet in October 2020. Its pitch is scalability through sharding — splitting the network into parallel pieces so transactions don't all queue behind each other — plus account names humans can read instead of strings of hex characters.

The NEAR token is what you're actually purchasing. It pays transaction fees, covers on-chain storage, and is staked by validators who help secure the network. That last part matters if you plan to hold: staking NEAR is possible, and it changes the economics of a long position.

## Exchange, wallet, or DEX: three doors into NEAR

There are three realistic routes, and they suit different people.

**Centralized exchange (CEX).** You register, verify your identity, fund with fiat or crypto, and buy NEAR/USDT on an order book. Fastest route if your money is currently in a bank account. You don't hold the private keys.

**Self-custody wallet.** Some wallets let you buy or swap NEAR directly. No exchange account, but you usually need crypto to start with, and you're responsible for your own seed phrase forever.

**Decentralized exchange (DEX).** On-chain swaps, no registration, but you pay gas and accept slippage. Best for people already living on-chain.

If this is your first NEAR purchase and your funds are sitting in a bank, the centralized exchange route is the practical one. Region matters here — exchanges can't serve everyone, and Gate lists the US, Canada, Iran, and Cuba among its restricted jurisdictions, so check Gate's user agreement before you do anything else.

## What a NEAR purchase actually costs

Now the part that decides whether you're buying well.

**The trading fee.** Gate's standard spot rate for a new account (VIP 0) is 0.10% maker and 0.10% taker. If you enable GT — Gate's native token — as your fee currency, that drops to 0.09%/0.09%. On a $500 NEAR order, that's the difference between $0.50 and $0.45. Real, but small.

**The funding fee.** This is where the money goes. A card purchase at 1–5% on $500 costs between $5 and $25. A bank transfer on the same amount might cost nothing on Gate's side and take a couple of days. A P2P trade has a 0% platform fee but a seller-set price that may include a built-in margin. If you're buying more than pocket change, funding method is the decision that matters, and the trading fee is a rounding error by comparison.

**The spread.** Market orders cross the bid-ask spread. On a liquid pair like NEAR/USDT that spread is narrow, but it isn't nothing, and it scales with order size. A limit order avoids paying it if you're willing to wait.

**Withdrawal fees.** Gate states that deposits are free, while withdrawal costs depend on live network congestion, so they move around.

The honest advice: if the bonus is the reason you picked a venue, you picked wrong. Bonuses are worth claiming once you've already decided the exchange fits your funding method and your region.

## Where to keep NEAR after you buy

Leaving NEAR on an exchange is convenient and carries counterparty risk. Gate has published proof-of-reserves reports since May 2020, verifiable via Merkle tree, which is a meaningful disclosure — and still not the same thing as holding your own keys.

The realistic options:

- **Exchange custody.** Easiest, supports trading and staking in one place, but you're trusting the platform. If you go this route, 2FA and an anti-phishing code aren't optional extras.
- **Gate's Web3 wallet.** Self-custodial, using MPC rather than a single seed phrase. Reasonable middle ground for people who want DeFi access without juggling raw keys.
- **Hardware wallet.** Offline key storage, works with Ledger, Trezor, and others. The right answer for a position you don't intend to touch, and the wrong answer if you plan to trade weekly.

If you withdraw, verify the network and address before sending anything, and send a small test amount first. On-chain transfers can't be reversed, and no support team can retrieve funds sent to the wrong address. Gate's own staff will never ask for your seed phrase or private key — anyone who does is running a scam.
